Temporal State Transport in Video Generation

Diagnosing and Correcting Spectral Imbalance

Reliable video generation requires more than high-quality individual frames. A model must transport identity, scene layout, motion, and fine details across time without drifting into fragmented transport or over-mixing.

Overview

A clean diagnosis of temporal failure, and a lightweight way to fix it.

We study video generation through the perspective of Temporal State Transport. Existing training-free methods often strengthen cross-frame interaction, but they do not reveal whether temporal attention stays in a healthy transport regime. Our view is that coherent video emerges when temporal attention is balanced: neither too fragmented nor excessively mixed.

To diagnose this structure, we introduce Spectral Tension, a signed quantity comparing local attention diffuseness with global spectral diversity. Based on that diagnosis, we propose Spectral Transport Homeostasis, a training-free query-temperature regulator that selectively corrects pathological temporal states while largely preserving balanced ones.
